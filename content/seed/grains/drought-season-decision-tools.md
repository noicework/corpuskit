# Seasonal Decision Tools for Drought-Affected Cropping Programs

## Executive summary

This briefing reviews decision points for grain growers managing a developing drought, including
when to cut losses on a failing crop, the opportunity cost of holding versus selling stored grain,
and cash flow modelling under three seasonal outlook scenarios. It draws on grower case studies
from the 2018-19 eastern states drought, one of the most severe on record across large parts of
NSW and Queensland. The central finding is that growers who used structured decision points made
earlier calls that preserved more equity than those who delayed decisions in hope of a seasonal
recovery that, in most case studies, did not eventuate.

## Background

Drought decision-making is complicated by genuine uncertainty about how a season will finish, and
by the understandable reluctance to lock in a loss on a crop that could still recover with timely
rain. Without structured decision points, this uncertainty commonly leads to delay, which the
case studies in this briefing show is itself a costly choice, as input and opportunity costs
continue to accrue while the probability of a profitable outcome from waiting typically declines.

## Method

Twenty-two grower case studies were tracked through the 2018-19 season, recording the decision
points growers faced, the information available to them at each point, and the eventual outcome.
Cash flow models were built for each case study under three seasonal outlook scenarios - median,
below-median and severely below-median rainfall - to compare recommended input timing against
what growers actually did.

## Findings

- Early cut-for-hay decisions, made before a crop was visibly beyond recovery, preserved more
  farm equity than holding failing crops through to a marginal grain harvest in the majority of
  case studies reviewed.
- Growers who delayed the cut-for-hay decision by more than three weeks past the point where
  agronomic indicators suggested recovery was unlikely captured, on average, a materially lower
  return than those who acted earlier.
- Cash flow modelling under a below-median rainfall scenario changed recommended input timing -
  typically deferring or reducing nitrogen and fungicide spend - for two-thirds of the case study
  growers relative to their original season-start plan.
- Growers holding stored grain from the previous season faced a genuine opportunity cost trade-off
  between selling into a rising drought-affected price and holding for on-farm feed use; case
  studies showed this decision was best made against a documented feed budget rather than price
  speculation alone.
- Growers who had a documented decision framework in place before the season began reported
  making decisions faster and with less second-guessing than those working through each decision
  from first principles as conditions developed.
- None of the 22 case study growers who deferred a clear cut-loss decision by more than a month
  reported a financial outcome better than the earlier-decision comparison group in the same
  district.

## Discussion

The value of structured decision tools in this briefing is less about predicting the season
accurately - none of the tools claim to forecast rainfall - and more about removing delay from
decisions that need to be made under uncertainty regardless. Case studies consistently showed
that acting on the information available at a defined decision point, even when that information
was incomplete, outperformed waiting for greater certainty that in a drought frequently never
arrives before the cost of waiting exceeds the cost of an imperfect early decision.

## Recommendations

1. Set defined decision points before the season begins - dates or agronomic triggers at which a
   cut-for-hay, input-reduction or grain-sale decision will be made - rather than deciding
   reactively as conditions develop.
2. Model cash flow under a below-median rainfall scenario at the start of the season, even in a
   season that begins with average conditions, so input timing decisions can be adjusted quickly
   if the season deteriorates.
3. Base stored grain hold-versus-sell decisions on a documented on-farm feed budget rather than
   price speculation, given the genuine and often underestimated value of feed security in a
   drought.
4. Treat delay itself as a cost. The case studies found no example where deferring a clear cut-
   loss decision by more than a month produced a better outcome than acting earlier on the same
   information.

## Conclusion

Structured decision tools do not remove the uncertainty inherent in a developing drought, but the
2018-19 case studies show that growers who used defined decision points and cash flow modelling
under a pessimistic scenario preserved more equity than those who delayed decisions while waiting
for greater certainty.

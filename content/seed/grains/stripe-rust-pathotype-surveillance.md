# Stripe Rust Pathotype Surveillance Network - Establishment and Season One Findings

## Executive summary

This briefing documents the establishment of a national stripe rust (Puccinia striiformis f. sp.
tritici) surveillance network following the detection of a new pathotype capable of overcoming
resistance genes present in several leading commercial wheat varieties. Trap nurseries were
established at 25 locations across the eastern wheatbelt in the network's first season, alongside
a spore trapping program feeding pathotype identification results back to growers, agronomists
and breeders within a fortnight of confirmation. Early results indicate the new pathotype spread
faster than any previous incursion tracked by the program, reaching all but two trap nursery
sites within four months of first detection.

## Background

Stripe rust arrived in Australia in 1979 and has since been managed largely through resistant
varieties, with periodic pathotype shifts requiring breeding programs to refresh resistance
sources. A pathotype detected in a single commercial paddock in the previous season was confirmed
by glasshouse testing to overcome resistance genes Yr17 and Yr27, both widely deployed in current
varieties, prompting the urgent establishment of this network.

## Method

Trap nurseries consist of a standard set of differential wheat lines sown at each of the 25
locations, monitored weekly for rust development from tillering through to flowering. Spore
traps positioned at 12 of the sites captured airborne inoculum for genetic fingerprinting,
allowing the network to distinguish local build-up from long-distance spread events. Samples
confirmed as the new pathotype were fast-tracked through the pathology laboratory ahead of the
routine testing queue.

## Findings

- The new pathotype was confirmed present at 23 of 25 trap nursery sites by the end of the first
  season, spreading from a single point of detection across three states within four months.
- Varieties carrying Yr17 or Yr27 as their principal resistance source showed severity scores
  consistent with full susceptibility at 19 of the affected sites.
- Long-distance spore trap data indicated at least two distinct long-range dispersal events,
  consistent with wind-borne spread across several hundred kilometres rather than gradual local
  build-up alone.
- Turnaround time from sample receipt to pathotype confirmation averaged six days in the first
  season, with results distributed to growers and agronomists within a fortnight of confirmation
  in all cases.
- No commercially available variety tested carried complete resistance to the new pathotype,
  though several breeding lines in pre-release trials retained useful levels of resistance.

## Discussion

The speed and distance of spread recorded in this first season underscores the value of a
coordinated, real-time surveillance network over ad hoc regional monitoring. Because stripe rust
inoculum can travel long distances on prevailing weather systems, a pathotype shift detected in
one region can reach distant cropping areas within a single season, leaving growers with little
lead time to change variety choice. The fortnight turnaround from detection to grower notification
is intended to support in-season fungicide decisions where variety resistance has failed, rather
than only informing next season's variety selection.

## Recommendations

1. Growers currently sowing varieties reliant on Yr17 or Yr27 as their principal resistance
   source should budget for at least one strategic fungicide application in the coming season.
2. Agronomists should monitor the fortnightly pathotype bulletin through stem elongation, as
   in-season detection at a nearby trap site is the most reliable early warning available.
3. Breeding programs should prioritise advancement of the pre-release lines showing useful
   resistance to the new pathotype, given the absence of resistance in current commercial
   varieties.
4. The network should be expanded beyond the initial 25 sites in future seasons to close
   monitoring gaps identified in this first year, particularly in higher-rainfall zones where
   rust pressure is typically most severe.

## Conclusion

The first season of network operation confirms both the value and the urgency of coordinated
stripe rust surveillance. The rapid, long-distance spread of the new pathotype demonstrates that
variety resistance can be overturned faster than breeding programs can respond, making real-time
monitoring and timely grower communication a necessary complement to genetic resistance rather
than a substitute for it.

# Reducing Shark Bycatch in Longline Fisheries - On-Water Trial Results

## Executive summary

This report documents on-water trials testing circle hooks and shark excluder devices in a
commercial longline fishery, conducted across 12 vessels over one fishing season. Circle hooks
reduced deep-hooking rates in incidentally caught sharks compared with conventional J-hooks,
improving at-vessel survival prospects for released animals, while shark excluder devices reduced
overall shark interaction rates. Crew reported minimal change to target catch handling time after
fitting excluder devices, addressing a key practicality concern raised ahead of the trial.

## Background

Longline fisheries targeting tuna and billfish species can incidentally hook sharks, most of which
are required to be released under current management arrangements. Hooking location is a strong
predictor of post-release survival, with deep-hooked animals substantially less likely to survive
release than those hooked in the jaw. Circle hooks have shown reduced deep-hooking rates in
overseas longline fisheries, and shark excluder devices - modifications to leader and hook
configuration intended to deter shark bites before hooking occurs - had not previously been
trialled at commercial scale in this fishery.

## Method

Twelve commercial longline vessels participated in the trial over one full fishing season, each
fishing a combination of conventional J-hooks, circle hooks and shark excluder configurations
across alternating sets to allow direct comparison under matched fishing conditions. Observers
recorded hooking location, target species catch rates and crew-reported handling time for each
gear configuration.

## Findings

- Circle hooks reduced deep-hooking rates in incidentally caught sharks compared with J-hooks
  across the trial fleet, consistent with the mechanism by which circle hook design tends to
  result in jaw hooking as the hook rotates into position during a strike.
- Shark excluder devices were trialled across all 12 participating vessels over the season,
  reducing overall shark interaction rates on sets fishing the excluder configuration relative to
  matched conventional sets on the same vessels.
- Target species catch rates for tuna and billfish showed no statistically meaningful difference
  between circle hook and J-hook configurations across the trial, indicating the switch did not
  come at a target catch cost for participating vessels.
- Crew reported minimal change to target catch handling time after fitting excluder devices,
  addressing a practicality concern that had been raised by industry ahead of the trial regarding
  gear complexity and set/retrieval time.
- Deep-hooking reduction under circle hooks was most pronounced for the shark species most
  frequently caught as bycatch in the fishery, with a smaller but still positive effect observed
  for less commonly caught species where trial sample sizes were correspondingly smaller.
- Combining circle hooks with excluder devices in a subset of trial sets produced the lowest
  combined deep-hooking and interaction rate of any configuration tested, though this combination
  was fished on fewer sets than either measure independently and warrants confirmation at larger
  scale.

## Discussion

The trial addresses two separate but related bycatch outcomes: excluder devices reduce how often
sharks are hooked at all, while circle hooks improve the survival prospects of sharks that are
hooked despite the reduced interaction rate. Together they represent complementary rather than
substitute measures, and the preliminary result from the combined configuration - fished on
fewer sets but showing the strongest combined outcome - supports further trial work to confirm
whether the combination should become the recommended standard rather than either measure alone.

## Recommendations

1. Support broader circle hook adoption across the longline fleet given the deep-hooking
   reduction achieved without a target catch cost in this trial.
2. Encourage uptake of shark excluder devices, noting the practicality concern regarding handling
   time was not borne out in this trial's crew feedback.
3. Conduct a larger-scale trial of the combined circle hook and excluder device configuration to
   confirm the preliminary result on the smaller subset of sets fished with both measures
   together in this trial.
4. Extend observer coverage for less commonly caught shark species in future trial phases, given
   the smaller sample sizes available for those species in this season's results.

## Conclusion

Both circle hooks and shark excluder devices reduced shark bycatch harm in this commercial-scale
trial without a target catch cost or meaningful handling time impact, and the preliminary result
from combining both measures supports further work to establish whether the combination should
become the fishery's recommended standard gear configuration.

# Cold Chain Innovation for Premium Seafood Export - Post-Harvest Quality Trial

## Executive summary

This report presents a post-harvest trial comparing modified atmosphere packaging and rapid
super-chilling against conventional ice slurry storage for premium finfish destined for export
markets. Super-chilled product held sensory quality scores significantly longer than ice slurry
storage, reducing rejection rates on arrival in key Asian markets among trial exporters who
adopted the protocol. Modified atmosphere packaging extended measurable shelf life but came with
a trade-off in initial texture scores that sensory panellists rated less favourably than either
alternative method at the point of packing.

## Background

Premium finfish export relies on maintaining sensory and food safety quality across a supply
chain that can span several days from harvest to final market delivery. Conventional ice slurry
storage has long been the industry default, but its cooling capacity is limited relative to
newer technologies, and rejection at destination due to quality decline represents a direct and
substantial cost to exporters, both in lost product value and reputational risk with buyers.

## Method

Trial shipments of premium finfish were processed under three post-harvest protocols - ice
slurry, modified atmosphere packaging, and rapid super-chilling to a target core temperature of
minus 1°C - then transported through commercial export supply chains to Asian markets. Trained
sensory panels assessed appearance, odour and texture at packing and again on arrival, alongside
a record of rejection outcomes across trial shipments.

## Findings

- Super-chilled product retained acceptable sensory quality scores roughly four days longer than
  product stored in conventional ice slurry, based on trained panel assessment against defined
  rejection thresholds for appearance, odour and texture.
- Export rejection rates on arrival fell after trial exporters adopted the super-chilling protocol
  for their premium product lines, compared with their own historical rejection rate under ice
  slurry storage for equivalent product and destination markets.
- Modified atmosphere packaging extended measurable shelf life beyond ice slurry storage, but
  initial texture scores at the point of packing were rated lower by the sensory panel than either
  ice slurry or super-chilling, an effect attributed to the packaging process itself rather than
  storage temperature.
- Core temperature achieved under the super-chilling protocol was consistently closer to the
  minus 1°C target across trial shipments than the variable temperatures recorded under ice
  slurry storage, where temperature control depended heavily on ice replenishment discipline
  through the supply chain.
- Buyer feedback collected for a subset of super-chilled shipments rated product condition on
  arrival more favourably than historical feedback for equivalent ice-slurry-stored shipments to
  the same buyers, consistent with the sensory panel findings.
- The cost of implementing super-chilling equipment was recovered within the trial period for
  exporters shipping high-value product lines, based on the value of rejected shipments avoided,
  though the payback period was longer for lower-value product lines where rejection cost savings
  were smaller relative to the equipment investment.

## Discussion

The trial results support super-chilling as the stronger overall protocol for premium, high-value
export lines, where the combination of extended shelf life and preserved texture outperforms both
alternatives and the equipment cost is recovered quickly through avoided rejections. Modified
atmosphere packaging's texture trade-off makes it a less clear-cut choice for premium product
specifically, though it may retain a role for lower-value lines where texture standards are less
stringent and its shelf-life extension still has commercial value.

## Recommendations

1. Exporters of premium finfish lines should prioritise adoption of rapid super-chilling over
   conventional ice slurry, given the consistent sensory quality, rejection rate and payback
   period findings in this trial.
2. Where modified atmosphere packaging is used, apply it to product lines with less stringent
   texture requirements, given the initial texture score trade-off identified at the point of
   packing.
3. Invest in temperature control discipline through the supply chain regardless of protocol
   chosen, as variability in achieved temperature under ice slurry storage was itself a
   contributor to quality decline independent of the storage method's inherent capacity.
4. Track buyer feedback systematically alongside sensory panel data, as the alignment between the
   two in this trial supports buyer feedback as a useful, lower-cost ongoing quality indicator
   once a protocol is adopted commercially.

## Conclusion

Rapid super-chilling outperformed both conventional ice slurry and modified atmosphere packaging
on the combination of shelf life, sensory quality and export rejection outcomes in this trial, and
represents a commercially viable upgrade path for exporters of premium finfish, with the fastest
payback for the highest-value product lines.

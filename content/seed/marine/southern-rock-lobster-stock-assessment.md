# Southern Rock Lobster Stock Assessment - Legal Size and Effort Modelling Update

## Executive summary

This report presents an updated stock assessment for southern rock lobster (Jasus edwardsii)
incorporating revised natural mortality estimates and a decade of tag-recapture data collected
since the previous full assessment. Effort modelling under three catch scenarios, reflecting
different plausible market demand outlooks, shows the current legal minimum length setting
maintains spawning biomass above the limit reference point under moderate fishing pressure, though
the high-demand scenario approaches the threshold within the ten-year projection window.

## Background

Southern rock lobster is a high-value export fishery managed primarily through a combination of
total allowable catch and legal minimum length settings intended to protect spawning biomass
ahead of any single season's catch. Stock assessments are periodically revised as tag-recapture
programs accumulate sufficient data to refine growth and natural mortality estimates that were
previously based on shorter time series or proxy data from related fisheries.

## Method

The assessment updated the stock model with a decade of tag-recapture data from 6,400 tagged
animals across the fishery, providing a longer and more geographically representative basis for
natural mortality and growth estimates than the previous assessment. Three effort scenarios -
low, moderate and high fishing pressure, calibrated to plausible export market demand outlooks -
were projected forward ten years to assess spawning biomass trajectory against the limit
reference point.

## Findings

- Ten years of tag-recapture data from 6,400 tagged animals produced a revised natural mortality
  estimate lower than the proxy value used in the previous assessment, indicating the stock is
  somewhat more resilient to fishing pressure than previously modelled.
- Under the moderate effort scenario, spawning biomass remained above the limit reference point
  throughout the ten-year projection, consistent with current catch settings being sustainable at
  present demand levels.
- Under the high-demand effort scenario, spawning biomass approached but did not breach the limit
  reference point within the ten-year projection window, indicating a reduced but not eliminated
  margin of safety if fishing pressure rises toward the upper end of the modelled range.
- The low effort scenario showed spawning biomass tracking well above the limit reference point
  and trending upward, suggesting current settings would allow for stock rebuilding if effort
  were to decline.
- Growth rate estimates derived from the tag-recapture data varied meaningfully by region within
  the fishery, with animals in the western part of the fishery reaching legal minimum length
  roughly one year later on average than those in the eastern part.
- The revised natural mortality estimate had a larger effect on the projected spawning biomass
  trajectory than any of the three effort scenarios individually, underscoring the value of the
  decade of tag-recapture data in reducing assessment uncertainty.

## Discussion

The regional variation in growth rate identified in the tag-recapture data has management
implications beyond the headline biomass finding: a uniform legal minimum length across the
fishery may allow proportionally more harvest of not-yet-spawned animals in the slower-growing
western region than in the east. This was not addressed by the current effort scenarios, which
modelled the fishery as a single unit, and represents an area the assessment recommends for
further work.

## Recommendations

1. Maintain current legal minimum length and catch settings, which remain consistent with
   spawning biomass staying above the limit reference point under moderate effort.
2. Monitor market demand indicators against the three modelled effort scenarios, given the
   narrower safety margin identified under the high-demand scenario, and be prepared to review
   settings if effort trends toward that scenario.
3. Investigate region-specific management settings given the identified difference in growth
   rate between the eastern and western parts of the fishery, as a uniform legal minimum length
   may not equally protect spawning potential across regions.
4. Continue the tag-recapture program to further refine natural mortality and growth estimates,
   given how materially the decade of data collected for this assessment reduced uncertainty
   relative to the previous proxy-based estimate.

## Conclusion

The updated assessment confirms current management settings are consistent with a sustainable
spawning biomass trajectory under moderate fishing pressure, while flagging a narrower safety
margin under a high-demand scenario and a previously unaddressed regional difference in growth
rate that warrants further investigation.

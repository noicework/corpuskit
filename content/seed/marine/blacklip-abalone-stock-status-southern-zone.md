# Blacklip Abalone Stock Status Assessment - Southern Zone

## Executive summary

This report presents a fishery-independent survey and catch-curve analysis of blacklip abalone
(Haliotis rubra) across the southern zone, updating biomass estimates following localised
recruitment failure linked to a marine heatwave event recorded two summers prior. Juvenile
recruitment in two southern sub-blocks fell to under 40% of the long-term average, while adjacent
sub-blocks outside the heatwave's core footprint showed recruitment within normal range. The
report recommends a precautionary reduction in total allowable catch for the two affected sub-
blocks pending clearer recovery signals in subsequent juvenile surveys.

## Background

Blacklip abalone is managed on a sub-block basis in the southern zone in recognition of the
species' limited mobility and correspondingly localised population dynamics. A marine heatwave
event two summers before this assessment produced sustained sea surface temperatures several
degrees above the long-term seasonal average across parts of the zone, coinciding with the
settlement period during which abalone recruitment is most sensitive to thermal stress.

## Method

Fishery-independent surveys were conducted at 85 permanent monitoring sites distributed across
all sub-blocks in the zone, using timed swim searches by trained divers recording abalone counts
by size class. Catch-curve analysis of commercial catch sampling data was used to estimate
fishing mortality by sub-block, and juvenile density at monitoring sites was compared against a
fifteen-year baseline to identify recruitment anomalies.

## Findings

- Juvenile recruitment (animals below legal minimum length) in two southern sub-blocks fell to
  under 40% of the fifteen-year average density, a decline consistent in timing and location with
  the marine heatwave's recorded thermal footprint.
- Adjacent sub-blocks outside the core heatwave footprint showed juvenile recruitment within the
  normal historical range, supporting a localised rather than zone-wide cause for the decline.
- Legal-size biomass in the two affected sub-blocks remained close to target reference levels at
  the time of survey, indicating the current commercial catch reflects recruitment from before
  the heatwave rather than an immediate biomass collapse.
- Catch-curve analysis estimated fishing mortality in the affected sub-blocks was within the
  target range, meaning the recruitment failure was not attributable to fishing pressure.
- Water temperature loggers deployed at monitoring sites recorded sustained temperatures more than
  2°C above the long-term seasonal average for over three weeks during the settlement period in
  the affected sub-blocks, a duration and magnitude consistent with settlement-stage mortality
  documented in comparable abalone heatwave events elsewhere.
- Modelling of the affected sub-blocks under current catch settings projects legal-size biomass
  declining below the limit reference point within four to six years as the reduced recruitment
  cohort reaches harvestable size, absent a catch adjustment.

## Discussion

The core management challenge identified in this assessment is a lag effect: current legal-size
biomass appears healthy because it reflects recruitment from before the heatwave, but the
recruitment failure identified in juvenile surveys will flow through to a biomass decline several
years from now unless catch is adjusted proactively. Waiting for the biomass decline to appear in
legal-size surveys before acting would forgo the lead time this juvenile survey data provides.

## Recommendations

1. Apply a precautionary reduction to total allowable catch in the two affected sub-blocks,
   sized to offset the projected recruitment-driven biomass decline before it reaches the legal-
   size population.
2. Maintain the current catch settings in sub-blocks outside the heatwave's core footprint, where
   recruitment remained within normal range.
3. Increase the frequency of juvenile density monitoring in the affected sub-blocks from the
   standard biennial cycle to annual, to detect a recovery signal as early as possible and support
   timely restoration of catch settings.
4. Retain water temperature logging at monitoring sites through future settlement periods, given
   the clear association identified here between thermal anomalies and recruitment failure.

## Conclusion

A distinct, spatially localised recruitment failure linked to a marine heatwave has been
identified in two southern zone sub-blocks, and a precautionary catch reduction in those sub-
blocks is recommended now to avoid a legal-size biomass decline that current survey data indicates
is otherwise likely within four to six years.

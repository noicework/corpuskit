Put your organisation’s research to work

CorpusKit is an open source research portal. Connect a research collection and people can search it, question it, browse its library and follow how the work connects – with every answer tied to passages they can open and check.

Apache 2.0 licence· Self-hosted on your infrastructure· Cited answers with plain-language confidence

Answers that show their working

Ask a question and the portal answers from the collection – streamed as it is produced, with numbered citations, follow-up questions and a plain statement of how well the sources support it. Each citation opens the passage it rests on, so checking a claim takes one click, and what you get is the exact lines from the research source.

Deep research breaks a broad question into focused sub-questions before answering it, and shows the reader that structure.

Sessions can be saved, renamed and exported, so a line of enquiry can be picked up where it was left.

When the whole collection is too wide, ask a cited question of one document from its page in the Library.

When the evidence is thin, it says so

Research rarely hands over one clean answer, and a portal that pretends otherwise teaches its readers to stop checking. So CorpusKit marks its weak moments in plain sight – that candour is what makes the strong answers worth trusting.

Search, the library and the map

Ask is one door into a collection. The rest of the portal is built for the days when you would rather look than ask.

Search shows what matched

Results arrive ranked and scored, each carrying the passage that made it relevant, so you can judge a result before you open it. A short cited answer sits above the results by default; hide it and work from the results alone whenever that suits the job better.

Hybrid, semantic and keyword modes, filters for topic and document kind, and watched searches that check for changed results daily.

A library set out for reading

Every resource is presented as a research item with a clear title, a summary, key takeaways and notable quotes, whatever the underlying file was called. Open one and the original document sits beside its extracted text, its related resources, and a cited conversation held against that document alone.

Search, sort and filter the whole catalogue, in grid or list view.

The shape of the collection

The knowledge map shows what the research is about: the entities in it, the relationships between them, and a concept view of topics that occur together. Inspect a connection and the portal opens the resources behind it, so even the map answers to its sources.

Search the map, filter it, and move between entity and concept views.

Run it yourself

CorpusKit is published under the Apache 2.0 licence, and the repository holds the whole application: shared types, retrieval integration, tests, build tasks and deployment configuration. Clone it, connect a collection and run the portal on infrastructure you choose.

One deployment can serve several organisation-specific portals from configuration alone, each with its own name, identity, topics and collection. Content storage and retrieval run on a connected knowledge service, so a deployment needs an account with one; the application itself is yours to run, change and redistribute.

git clone https://github.com/corpuskit/corpuskit.git
cd corpuskit
cp .env.example .env   # add knowledge service credentials
deno task dev
Portal running at http://localhost:8790

The short version. The repository documents the full setup, containers included.

Help look after it

CorpusKit is developed in the open, and the people who run portals are often the people improving them. Code, design, documentation and tests all take contributions, and the standard is the same for everyone: a change says what it does, carries tests where behaviour changes, and passes the checks every other change passes.

If you are new to the project, start small and concrete: report a problem with enough detail to reproduce it, improve a page of documentation you found wanting, or pick up an issue marked for first-time contributors. Not sure where something belongs? Open an issue and ask.

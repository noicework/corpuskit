# Creative brief – the CorpusKit home page

You are building the home page for CorpusKit in one pass. This brief sets direction; the decisions are yours. Wherever I offer a possibility, treat it as raw material to take or discard, not an instruction. The one document you must treat as law is the brand identity, `brand-codex.md` – read it whole before you open an editor. Every line in it survived heavy owner review, and every line of copy on this page comes from you, written in that voice. The brand idea it all hangs off: put your organisation's research to work.

## What this page is

CorpusKit is an open source research portal – search, cited answers, a browsable library, a knowledge map, all built so the reader can check what they find. This page is the project's front door, and the person at the door is not a buyer. They might use a portal someone runs, they might be the engineer deciding whether their organisation should run it, they might want to contribute. This is an open source project's home, not a pitch.

Hold in your head how tailwindcss.com or wordpress.org meet you: they show the thing working, tell you what it is, and let you get on with your day. Nobody follows you around the page. That is the posture. There is no hard sell anywhere on this page – not softened sell, not sell dressed as friendliness. The confidence of the page is that the product, shown honestly, is enough.

## The direction: the reading room

Picture a good library reading room on a weekday morning. Daylight on long tables. Papers out and being worked on, not behind glass. Everything within reach, everything checkable, and nobody selling anything, because a reading room has nothing to sell – it simply works, and it has clearly been looked after for a long time. That is the page. A working room, not a hushed one: the brand idea is research put to work, so the papers are open and marked up, not filed away.

The name should need no decoding, and if a choice on the page would feel wrong in that room – a flashing sign, a sales banner, a wall painted to grab attention – it is wrong on the page.

### Colour

Head towards paper and ink. Grounds in a warm white – paper that has seen daylight, not the blue-white of a hospital corridor. Text in a deep near-black ink that feels written rather than printed by a machine. Then one working accent, used the way a careful reader uses a pen: to mark what matters, and for nothing else. If the accent shows up somewhere a reader's pen wouldn't, it is decoration, and it goes.

For the accent's territory, reason from the world of marking and checking evidence – the blue of good pen ink and of a citation you can follow has an honesty to it, and so does the ochre of an archival folder or a fine highlighter used sparingly. Choose the hue yourself, for a reason you could say out loud. Two territories are closed: the current site's forest green, orange and lime, in any weight, and the purple-into-indigo gradient haze that every AI product currently hides in. CorpusKit's brand is candour; a gradient haze is the visual opposite of candour.

### Typography

This page will mostly be read, so choose type the way a serious journal chooses it – for reading. The tell of the tier we are aiming at is type that looks set, by someone who reads, rather than styled from a template. Headlines need a face with real character that stays calm at large sizes – large and quiet, never large and shouting. That could be a serif with genuine bookish authority or a humanist sans with warmth; the choice is yours, but be able to say why the face belongs in the reading room. Body type should read like excellent documentation: unremarkable in the best sense, effortless at length.

Monospace is honest exactly where real code appears – this project genuinely asks people to clone and run it, and a line of code shown as code builds trust. The old site wore monospace as costume, in uppercase label lines; decorative mono is a tell of the site we are leaving behind, so keep mono strictly on duty.

## The feeling

The reader should leave thinking: this is careful work, I can see exactly what it does, I could run this, and nobody tried to sell it to me. Trust arrived through evidence, not claims – the same way the product itself earns it.

The page must never feel like being marketed at. Never like an AI product launch, never like a pitch deck with scroll effects, never like a toy or a demo reel. The moment a reader feels a hand on their back steering them towards a button, we have lost them – these are people who read licences for a living.

## What the page must do

These are jobs, not sections. How you group, order and count them is yours.

**Make the capability felt, evidence first.** The centre of the page is what CorpusKit can actually do, shown doing it: search that reveals the passages that matched, answers that carry numbered citations and plain-language confidence, a library of real research items, a knowledge map with shape and relationships. Shown means shown – the reader should see the product behave, not read adjectives about it. The proof points section of the brand document is your claim ledger: if a capability is not in it, the page does not claim it.

And show the candour, because it is the whole point. This product labels weak matches, tells you when confidence is low and to check the sources, and can say the collection holds no direct evidence for an answer. Every other product hides those moments; for CorpusKit they are the proof of the idea, and shown plainly they are worth more than any headline. If you want a candidate for the page's most memorable beat, the instant an answer proves itself – a claim opening into the passage behind it – is the entire brand in one gesture. Equally strong, and rarer: the product declining to overclaim. Yours to take or discard.

One boundary here: the portals CorpusKit currently runs are not public. Never name them, never link them, and make sure nothing shown could identify them. Whatever content demonstrates the capability must stand on its own.

**Answer the person working out whether to run it.** Somewhere on this page, the engineer with the deciding vote gets what they came for: the licence, stated plainly as Apache 2.0 when the detail matters; the repository, one obvious step away; what a deployment actually needs, including that content storage and retrieval run on a connected knowledge service – the brand document draws that boundary precisely, keep to it. They should also catch the scent of stewardship: a project that names what is unfinished, says how changes are checked, and reads like infrastructure someone maintains rather than a launch someone shipped. For the person who wants to contribute, the welcome is specific and honest about the standard – an open door, not a recruitment drive.

**End the page deliberately.** The bottom of a page is where most sites trail off into a stacked banner of last-chance buttons. This one ends the way a good essay ends – composed, on purpose, leaving the reader with their next step held out plainly: use it, run it, or contribute to it. The last screen deserves the same art direction as the first. A reader who scrolls to the end has given you their full attention; the ending should be worthy of it.

## The quality bar

Premium and art-directed, with calm confidence. The three Caitlyn screenshots in this folder are the tier to reach or beat. Study what makes them feel expensive: enormous restraint, one idea per screen, whitespace doing the work decoration usually does, a single textured illustration carrying more than ten icons would. Then take the restraint and leave the look – Caitlyn is a closed product pitching itself; we are an open project showing itself. Same tier, different soul.

Motion: the page rides ScrollSmoother's damped scroll – that weight and glide is the given foundation. On top of it, a small number of considered moments, and no more. One signature moment beats twenty effects; restraint is the mark of quality here, and earlier builds of this page were rejected partly for too much animation. Most of the page should simply sit there, composed, confident enough to hold still. When you are unsure whether something should move, it shouldn't.

Quality also means the page holds everywhere: on a phone, on a big monitor where it should breathe rather than strand itself in a narrow column, and for a reader with system text scaled up. A page that is premium only at one width on one machine is not premium.

Three quick taste tests while you work. Would a design-literate stranger screenshot this for a collection? Does the type look set rather than styled? Would a sceptical CTO trust the organisation that made it? If any answer is no, stop and fix that before moving on.

## The anti-reference

`corpuskit-desktop.png` is the current site, and it is the one thing this page cannot resemble. Its forest green, orange and lime; its heavy grotesk headlines ending in full stops; its uppercase mono label lines above every heading; its dark numbered strips and grid-paper backgrounds; its shape and structure. None of it carries over. If a choice you have just made would sit comfortably on that page, choose again.

## Hard rules

These pass through to the page without exception:

- Australian English throughout
- Sentence case headings, and no full stops ending headings
- No ampersands
- No em dashes – use a spaced en dash instead
- Plain language, no AI copy cliches, and the brand document's avoided-words list applies to every word on the page
- No eyebrows – no kicker label lines above headings, anywhere, in any styling
- Exactly one sign-in affordance for anonymous visitors
- Never name or link the client portals
- Nothing that resembles the current site

## What is yours

So there is no doubt about ownership: the page structure and section count are yours. Every line of copy is yours, written in the brand voice from the brand document – nothing in this brief is copy. The layout is yours. The final typefaces and exact colours are yours – I have given territory and reasons, and the tokens are your call. Motion detail is yours, including which moment becomes the signature. Illustration and diagram treatment is yours. Where I have offered candidates, discard them freely the moment you have something better – you are the one in the room with the work.

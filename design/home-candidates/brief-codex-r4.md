# Creative brief for the CorpusKit home page

## The assignment

Create a new home page for CorpusKit, the open source research portal.

The approved brand idea is **Put your organisation’s research to work**. Use it as the test for every decision. The page should make research feel active and useful: something people can search, question, browse, connect and check against its sources.

This is a project home, not a sales page. It should help someone understand the product by seeing it work, then let them choose what to do next without being pushed.

## Creative direction

### Research in working order

The visual world should feel like entering a beautifully maintained research workspace where the important material is already open and ready to inspect.

Picture a clear table, marked passages, linked references and a collection whose structure has become visible. It is careful without feeling precious, technical without looking like a developer console and serious without borrowing the ceremonial language of universities or government reports.

The page should make order visible. It should show that CorpusKit does more than produce an answer: it keeps the answer, its citations, the supporting passages, the confidence and the wider collection within reach.

There is a useful tension at the heart of the identity:

- Research is complex, but the interface is understandable
- Answers are useful, but never detached from evidence
- The project is capable, but candid about limits
- The product is composed, but open to inspection and change
- Each portal can have its own identity, while the underlying system remains dependable

Let that tension create the character. Avoid making the product look either bureaucratic or magical.

## The feeling to create

A reader should leave feeling that CorpusKit is real, capable and inspectable.

Someone using a portal should be able to imagine finding useful material without learning a new research method first. Someone responsible for a collection should recognise that the work of maintaining, organising and improving it has been taken seriously. Someone considering a deployment should feel that the project respects their technical judgement. A potential contributor should feel invited into a project with visible standards rather than a vague promise of community.

The page should create curiosity through evidence. It should make people want to open a source, follow a relationship or inspect the repository because they have already seen enough to understand why that action would be useful.

It must never feel like:

- A black-box answer machine
- An enterprise software pitch
- A chatbot landing page
- A glossy concept for a product that may not exist
- A consultancy site hiding the product behind claims
- An academic archive that expects patience before offering value
- A conversion funnel with open source language applied afterwards

Confidence should come from what is shown, not from inflated language.

## Put the capability at the centre

The centre of the page is CorpusKit working with a research collection.

Show enough of the real product behaviour that a reader can grasp the relationship between a question, an answer and its evidence. Citations, matched passages, confidence, weak evidence and the ability to open the underlying material are not supporting details. They are the reason the capability is trustworthy.

Let the wider shape of the product become apparent through genuine behaviours: searching in different ways, browsing the Library, working within one resource, exploring relationships, maintaining a collection and giving approved tools read-only access. These do not need equal prominence. The design should reveal a coherent system rather than parade a catalogue of features.

Find one memorable way to make verification visible. The signature moment should help the reader understand how a claim leads back to evidence or how a collection becomes navigable. It should clarify the product in a way a static marketing illustration could not.

Use actual interface language and credible research material wherever product detail appears. Avoid decorative pseudo-documents, meaningless charts and invented activity that exists only to fill a frame.

Candour is part of the demonstration. CorpusKit can show low confidence, weak matches and an absence of direct evidence. These states should feel like evidence of rigour, not embarrassing exceptions to hide.

## Open source posture

Take the posture of a strong open source project: show the thing, state what exists and give people a direct route into the work.

Tailwind CSS and WordPress are useful references for this posture, not for their visual styles. They meet people at different levels of familiarity, demonstrate capability early and make documentation, code and participation feel like natural continuations of the product.

There should be no hard sell, lead capture logic or artificial urgency. Do not frame self-hosting as freedom from villains or open source as moral superiority. Explain the practical autonomy it gives an organisation.

A person deciding whether to run CorpusKit should be able to establish:

- What the application does today
- That the application code is available under Apache 2.0
- That they can inspect, run and change it
- That the repository includes the application, shared types, tests, build work and deployment configuration
- That deployment requires a separately connected knowledge service
- How the project is maintained and how changes are checked
- Where to inspect the code, understand setup, report a problem or contribute
- Which limits, dependencies and unfinished areas matter to a responsible decision

Treat governance and operational boundaries as part of trust. They should not be buried beneath promotional reassurance.

Do not name or link any non-public organisation portal. Existing private deployments are not public examples.

## Visual character

Leave the current CorpusKit site behind completely.

Do not retain its institutional green identity, graph-paper background, acid accents, heavy dark grotesque headlines, outlined information panels, numbered feature band or portal-card showcase. The new page needs a different colour world, different typographic voice and different underlying composition. A visitor familiar with the current page should not mistake this for a reskin.

Move towards the colours of research materials and working tools rather than a conventional technology brand. Warm paper, carbon ink, photocopy grey, document blue, annotation red, faded tab colours and the occasional sharper signal all belong in the world. Choose a dominant colour relationship that makes links, citations and active evidence immediately legible. Colour should indicate purpose as often as it creates atmosphere.

Avoid a rainbow of product categories. CorpusKit is one connected instrument. Restrained colour, used decisively, will make evidence states and the signature interaction more meaningful.

Typography should bring the texture of reading into a modern product setting. Consider letterforms with some literary or archival character for the larger voice, supported by type that stays exceptionally clear inside interface material. The result should feel good around report titles, citations, technical details and plain-language explanations. Avoid both the current site’s compressed, blunt display weight and the generic oversized geometric typography common to software launches.

Type should create authority through proportion and rhythm, not sheer scale. The Caitlyn references demonstrate confidence, air and disciplined composition, but do not follow their sparse startup structure or turn the page into a sequence of enormous slogans.

## Composition and imagery

Treat the page as an art-directed whole rather than a stack of interchangeable website blocks.

Use space to establish relationships between the research, the answer and the evidence. Dense product material can sit beside areas of calm, much as a marked-up document rests on an otherwise clear desk. Repetition should build understanding rather than reveal a component library.

The most valuable imagery is the product behaving truthfully. Interface fragments may be reframed, enlarged, layered or brought into conversation with source material, provided the relationship remains understandable. The goal is not a literal tour of every screen. It is to let someone see the system’s habits.

If additional visual material is used, keep it rooted in the physical traces of research: annotations, index marks, tabs, passages, diagrams, document edges and connections. Avoid stock photography of offices, glowing brains, abstract data clouds, floating glass panels and synthetic three-dimensional objects with no relationship to the work.

The finish should match or exceed the Caitlyn references in restraint, confidence and attention to proportion. CorpusKit should have more substance on the page because the product has more to demonstrate, but that substance must remain composed.

## Motion

Use ScrollSmoother to give the page a damped, settled scroll feel.

Most of the page should simply sit there and hold its composition. Motion should be rare enough that each use has meaning. Let one signature sequence carry the expressive weight, ideally by revealing a relationship that matters to the product: answer to citation, citation to passage, question to collection or item to knowledge map.

Other movement should support orientation, continuity or direct interaction. Avoid constant entrances, wandering objects, staggered text, parallax applied to every layer and effects that make the reader wait for content. Restraint is part of the premium quality bar.

The page should remain convincing without motion. Reduced-motion behaviour should preserve the idea and hierarchy rather than feel like a diminished version.

## Jobs the page must do

These are outcomes, not a proposed page structure. They may overlap, recur or be solved in ways that do not resemble conventional sections.

- Make the basic capability understandable through use rather than assertion
- Show why cited answers are different from unsupported answers
- Keep source passages, confidence and evidence quality close to the claims they qualify
- Reveal that CorpusKit supports several ways into a collection, including asking, searching, browsing and following relationships
- Show that a portal is maintained infrastructure, with tools for stewardship rather than a one-off search interface
- Help an organisation judge the code, licence, dependencies, governance and practical route to running the project
- Give contributors a credible route into the repository and current work
- Distinguish clearly between the open source application and separately connected services or research material
- Give existing users one clear sign-in affordance
- End with intention, leaving the reader with useful routes forward rather than a generic promotional footer or repeated pitch

## Writing direction

The builder will write every line of page copy from the approved brand document.

Start from working capability. Use plain verbs such as find, ask, compare, open, save, check, run and contribute. Keep evidence close to the claim it supports. Name limits and dependencies directly. Refer to CorpusKit or the portal as doing the work, not a model or retrieval engine.

Use Australian English throughout.

All headings use sentence case and do not end in full stops. Do not use ampersands. Do not use em dashes; use a spaced en dash where that punctuation is needed.

Do not use kicker labels or small category lines above headings. There are no eyebrows anywhere on the page.

Avoid AI copy clichés, grand claims and language that turns uncertainty into theatre. Do not call CorpusKit a chatbot, copilot, intelligent platform, enterprise solution or single source of truth. Do not describe cited answers as definitive or authoritative. Do not substitute free for open source.

The copy should sound like a careful maintainer explaining a useful piece of software to another capable person.

## Product and access guardrails

Anonymous visitors should encounter one sign-in affordance across the page. Do not repeat it in the navigation, body and ending.

Sign-in is for people who already have access. It is not the primary story and should not compete with understanding the project.

Do not imply that every connected service, deployment cost or research collection is covered by the Apache 2.0 licence. The licence applies to the CorpusKit application code.

Do not make claims beyond the proof points in the approved brand document. If a product state is shown, it should correspond to something that exists.

## Quality bar

The page should feel premium because every element has a reason to be present.

Pay particular attention to typographic fit, text wrapping, the credibility of product material, image treatment, responsive composition and the relationship between stillness and the signature moment. The result should feel authored at every viewport, not like a desktop composition compressed for smaller screens.

Clarity and accessibility belong to the visual quality. Evidence links, states, confidence and interaction should remain understandable without depending on colour, fine print or animation alone.

A strong final page will feel calm on first view, richer on inspection and straightforward when someone decides to act.

## What belongs to the builder

The page structure and section count are deliberately yours.

You will decide the layout, hierarchy, responsive behaviour, exact visual devices, final colour values, typefaces, type scale, spacing, component treatment and motion detail. You will also write every line of copy in the approved voice.

Choose what to omit as carefully as what to include. Interpret the direction with your own judgement. The requirement is not to reproduce a wireframe that has already been imagined. It is to create the clearest, most distinctive expression of CorpusKit as a working, inspectable open source research portal.
